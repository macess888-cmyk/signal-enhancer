The deeper governance problem may not be intelligence.

It may be executability.

World models generate possibilities.

Reasoners generate plans.

Policies generate actions.

But none of those answer the same question:

“Should this proposed transition become executable at all?”

Across AI governance, runtime safety, validator drift, autonomous systems, and physical AI, the same pressure keeps appearing:

Not:

Can the system act?

But:

Does legitimate authority still exist when consequence is about to bind?

Different domains.

Same uncertainty.

When reality keeps generating the same question independently, it may be signaling a load-bearing invariant rather than a local problem.

The recurring failure mode may not be unauthorized execution.

It may be authority drift that goes unnoticed until execution occurs.

Most governance systems ask:

“Was this action permitted?”

The harder question is:

“Does the basis for permission still exist under current conditions?”

Authority can be valid at authorization.

Invalid at bind-time.

And still appear procedurally correct.

The gap between those two moments may be where some of the most expensive failures originate.
