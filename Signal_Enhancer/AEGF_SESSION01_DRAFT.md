## 1. What must be governed before execution?

Before execution, the formation conditions through which authority, permissions, constraints, legitimacy, and admissibility become possible must be governed.

Many governance failures appear to emerge long before execution occurs. By the time execution becomes visible, the conditions that made execution possible may already have formed, drifted, relocated, or become invisible.

For me, governance begins not with execution, but with the formation conditions that shape what becomes executable.

---

## 2. What governance problem does your work address?

My work explores pressure relocation and inspection surfaces.

One recurring observation is that constraints, dependencies, legitimacy pressures, trust relationships, and preservation priorities often do not disappear when they weaken or fail.

They relocate.

The governance challenge is that most systems are designed to inspect visible constraints but are often unable to detect where those pressures have moved.

This can allow governance-relevant transitions to occur long before consequence becomes visible.

---

## 3. Where does your work sit in the transition chain?

Evidence -> Trust -> Authority -> Permission -> Action -> Consequence

The HACR Hybrid Observatory primarily operates in the transitions between Evidence, Trust, and Authority.

The Observatory does not grant authority.

It does not determine admissibility.

It does not authorize execution.

Its role is to inspect how signals, pressures, dependencies, relationships, and standing evolve around those transitions.

---

## 4. What must happen before execution?

Before execution:

* Formation conditions should be visible.
* Sources of authority should be localized.
* Constraints should be inspectable.
* Admissibility conditions should be identifiable.
* Relationship fields should be observable.
* Pressure relocation should be detectable.
* Governance-relevant transitions should be reviewable.

The goal is not simply to determine what may execute.

The goal is to understand how execution became admissible in the first place.

---

## 5. What should remain auditable afterward?

After execution, systems should retain visibility into:

* Evidence lineage
* Authority lineage
* Constraint lineage
* Admissibility conditions
* Decision pathways
* Pressure relocation
* Relationship-field evolution
* Consequence formation

Auditability should allow reconstruction of how a trajectory became possible, not merely what happened after execution occurred.

---

## 6. What remains unresolved?

One unresolved question continues to stand out:

Can governance-relevant standing form around an object before anyone recognizes that a governance transition has occurred?

The object may remain unchanged.

The execution boundary may remain unchanged.

Yet the surrounding relationship field may evolve in ways that alter how the object is trusted, relied upon, inherited, preserved, or protected.

If so, governance transitions may occur long before authority changes, execution changes, or consequence becomes visible.

Understanding how relationship fields evolve, how standing forms, and how those transitions can be inspected before they become governance-relevant remains an active area of exploration.
